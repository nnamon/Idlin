# 0027: How chat joins and commands citizens

Date: 2026-11-27. Decided by the designer (Mythos 5.2) for
[#337](https://github.com/nnamon/Idlin/issues/337), where the operator asked
for a design of how chat joins and controls citizens; open to veto.
[chat.md](../chat.md) is the design. It follows the operator's rules on
[#331](https://github.com/nnamon/Idlin/issues/331): one chatter is one
citizen, with no join command and no bot residents, and everyone is on one
screen. It also follows the operator's scope for now: no programs submitted
by chatters, and only the streamer has access to the site.

0025 is on `main`, and open pull requests carry another 0025 and two 0026s,
so this takes 0027, the first number none of them has.

| Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Rejected alternative                                                                                                                                           | Why                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A chat command changes only what a citizen's program reads: its flag, a `param`, its program, its vote.                                                                                                                                                                     | Direct verbs (`!attack`, `!eat`), or an override that suspends the program for a few seconds.                                                                  | With direct verbs the fastest typist wins and the program is what runs when nobody types. A rule that reads the flag does what an override would, and "turn back" can outrank it.               |
| A program declares its own commands: every `param` is a chat command, with a range or named values. The game has five built-in commands.                                                                                                                                    | Stances such as `!fight` and `!careful` built into the game.                                                                                                   | Another preset can then replace the vocabulary, and adding one changes nothing in the gate or the parser.                                                                                       |
| Chatters steer through chat alone: `!go`, `!home`, `!free`, the params of the preset they run, votes, and `!run <preset>` among the presets the city offers. The site is the streamer's: settings, moderation, and which presets are on offer.                              | A companion page for each chatter with sliders and a program editor, paired to the account by a code typed in chat; programs written or published by chatters. | The operator's scope for now. It also removes the takeover a pairing code allowed, where one chatter posts their own code and others type it.                                                   |
| The stream shows whether a program took an order: a solid flag when a rule that reads it wins, or when a rule above the winner read it and did not hold; an outline and the winner's label only when the winner sits above every rule that reads the flag.                  | Showing only that the order was received; or calling a flag held whenever the winning rule does not read it.                                                   | It is the display that makes two programs differ in public. A citizen chopping beside the flag it walked to must not look like one that refused.                                                |
| A chatter's first message brings their citizen up through a hatch on the deck within 1.2 seconds, in every phase.                                                                                                                                                           | A crane onto the landing; or boarding at the next landing, a wait of minutes.                                                                                  | The deck is on screen in the city and landing views and is safe ground. The margin rule already keeps a late arrival aboard.                                                                    |
| A citizen stays awake for 24 hours after its chatter last typed, then goes below with its record kept. Present means typed in the last 20 minutes. With 150 awake, the longest-quiet citizen on deck gives up its berth, and never one who is present.                      | Out on landings for 3 days and aboard for 14, as the walking-city draft has it; or below after one quiet cycle.                                                | Fourteen days fills the berths with absent chatters on the shipped program, which is bot residents under usernames. One cycle ends idle play and empties the city when chat is slow.            |
| Speech is drawn as bubbles: 40 characters, 2.5 to 4 seconds, six at once, a speech tick for the rest. It passes through a tick as an event and is not in the hash.                                                                                                          | A chat log panel; or speech stored in the world.                                                                                                               | Every stream has a chat panel beside it. Text in the world grows snapshots and ties the hash to the filter list.                                                                                |
| The adapter is read-only, and every answer to a command is drawn on the stream.                                                                                                                                                                                             | A bot that replies in chat.                                                                                                                                    | A bot needs an account and a secret and posts on a third party's service, which the mandate rules out.                                                                                          |
| Orders and param changes cost a token: two held, one back every 20 seconds, counted in ticks inside the simulation.                                                                                                                                                         | Wall-clock cooldowns in the adapter.                                                                                                                           | A blow takes half a second and a sortie 90, so chat can redirect a sortie and cannot steer a fight. Counted in ticks, a capture with a scripted crowd repeats.                                  |
| The queue holds at most one waiting input of each kind for an account (13 at the most), a newer one replacing it in place.                                                                                                                                                  | Bounding only speech, with tokens checked when an input is applied.                                                                                            | Refused orders would still fill the queue and a tick's 32 places. Bounded, 30 flooding accounts take under half of them, and no input waits more than 6.3 seconds.                              |
| City-wide questions take one equal vote from each citizen that has been through a lift, cast as `!1` to `!3`. Chat cannot delay the lift.                                                                                                                                   | Votes weighted by level or tally; programs that vote; a vote to hold the ramps; `!go a` as the vote.                                                           | The oldest citizens, or the absent ones, would decide everything, and the margin means something only if the city leaves on time. `!go` already plants the flag.                                |
| The gate checks a command's shape and the simulation resolves its names at the tick it is applied. Inputs are logged one line a tick beside each snapshot.                                                                                                                  | Resolving names before the log.                                                                                                                                | The log then holds what was typed, and a replay from any snapshot resolves it the same way.                                                                                                     |
| A live chat's log stays in the server's data directory. Everything committed or posted uses scripted crowds.                                                                                                                                                                |                                                                                                                                                                | The repo is public, and a log holds usernames and what people typed.                                                                                                                            |
| When cities meet, each chat commands its own citizens, speech does not cross between streams, and in a raid on a copy the copied citizens run on their presets and standing params alone. A citizen on the wrong deck when two cities part is stripped and back on its own. | Shared speech; commands across cities; a captive on the rival's deck, as the walking-city draft has it.                                                        | A streamer cannot moderate the other chat. A city's defence while its chat sleeps is then the presets its chatters chose and the params they set. Every citizen stays on its own city's screen. |
| The turn-back sensor is `lift_eta`, the walking-city draft's name for it.                                                                                                                                                                                                   | A name of this page's own, `time_left`.                                                                                                                        | The sensor belongs to the cycle, which the plan owns.                                                                                                                                           |

Checked for this: the timings in chat.md are arithmetic from tick counts
(steps of 4 to 6 ticks, blows of 5), and `step(world, inputs)` in
`packages/sim/src/world.ts` already applies a list of inputs before a tick.
Not checked: anything on screen, the six-bubble and five-row limits at 150
citizens, and what Twitch's chat sends, which is from memory and is checked
in the adapter's issue.

Left to the plan: what a city with nobody awake looks like, how landings
scale under 15 citizens, and the lengths of the phases.
