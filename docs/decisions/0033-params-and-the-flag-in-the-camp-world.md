# 0033: Params and the flag in the camp world

Date: 2026-11-28. Decided for #405, slice piece 9 (chat C5, #351, and C6,
#352), open to veto. [chat.md](../chat.md), sections 3 and 8, and section 5
of [slice.md](../slice.md) give params, the flag and tokens. They are built in
`packages/sim/src/orders.ts`, the compiler and the machine, on the camp world,
which has no deck, cycle or ground levels yet. These choices were made where
the spec was silent or the camp world got in the way.

| Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Rejected alternative                                                     | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `lift_eta` reads nightfall in the camp: sighted 3,000 ticks before 19:00, 0 through the night. `way_home` is the walk to `camp`.                                                                                                             | Leaving `margin` and "turn back" until piece 2 builds the cycle.         | #352's held flag is shown under a winning "turn back", and margin means nothing without a clock to turn back by. Nightfall is when monsters come out, so it is the camp's nearest thing to the horde. The sensor names are the slice's, so programs keep them. |
| Deckhand declares all six of section 5's params; only `stance` and `margin` are read. `keep`, `over`, `aid` and `ramp` are kept on the citizen and set from chat, and no rule reads them yet. There is no "too deep" rule.                   | Stand-ins for ground levels, drops, downs and the ramp.                  | Each needs something the camp does not have. The slice's "flag on ground past `over` is held" test goes with piece 3, which builds `ground_level`. Declaring the params now gives chat its whole vocabulary and the snapshot its final shape.                  |
| A citizen keeps its account. `join` from an account that has a citizen only marks it heard; a name another citizen has is refused.                                                                                                           | Finding a citizen by name, as `join` did, and leaving the account to C3. | Every order is "from that citizen's own chat account" (chat.md, section 10). By name, a second chatter with the same name in other capitals would steer someone else's citizen.                                                                                |
| Whether the program took its flag is worked out from the bytecode: the first rule with a flag sensor in it. Followed is the winning rule at or below it, held above it, unread when there is none.                                           | Recording at run time which sensors a decision read.                     | chat.md defines held as the winner sitting above every rule that reads the flag, which is a fact about the program. Read off the code, it costs nothing per decision and cannot be fooled by short-circuiting.                                                 |
| Tokens are stored as a count and the tick the next one comes back, brought up to date when an order is applied.                                                                                                                              | Counting every citizen's tokens up each tick.                            | The same answers with no work on ticks where nobody types.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| A compass point is the walkable tile nearest the point 12 tiles from `camp` that way.                                                                                                                                                        | Refusing compass points until there are sectors.                         | The crowd files and chatters type `!go north`. 12 tiles lands inside the camp's region or at the mouth of the next one.                                                                                                                                        |
| `!go` looks places up by their names in the maps, typed with spaces for underscores.                                                                                                                                                         | The labels the viewer letters ("the Hills").                             | The labels are the client's and some places have none. The names are what programs use and what the crowd files type.                                                                                                                                          |
| Snapshot version 12 saves the orders. A version 11 snapshot loads with no account on anyone. A content change that alters a program puts its citizens' params back to the defaults, and brings down a flag on a citizen that was removed.    | Carrying params across by name when a program changes.                   | A snapshot keeps the values and not the params they belonged to, so a carried value could land on another param. Carrying by name belongs with `!run` (C12), which knows both programs.                                                                        |
| A version 11 world that held chat citizens keeps them as plain residents with no account. A chatter whose name one of them has gets no citizen in that world, because `join` refuses a name in use. Such a world is started afresh for chat. | Handing a v11 citizen to the first account that joins under its name.    | No world with chat citizens has run long enough to keep: chat came in with #385 and the live world runs house residents. A hand-over by name would bring back the name-based steering this decision removes.                                                   |
