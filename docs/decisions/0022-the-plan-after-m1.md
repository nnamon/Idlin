# 0022: The plan after M1

Date: 2026-11-27. Decided by the design lead on #313, from three designers'
proposals there; open to veto. The plan itself is [plan.md](../plan.md), and
its section 2 lists every conflict between the proposals and how it was
settled. These are the decisions that change something already written down.
[Decision 0023](0023-what-an-owner-sees.md) has the ones that came from the
fun audit of the plan: the order of the milestones, what was cut, and what an
owner is shown as the world grows.

| Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Rejected alternative                                                                                   | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Builds and owners are one milestone, M2, built as two strands from one shared foundation issue (programs as world state, and a log of inputs).                                                                         | Trades at M2 and players at M3, as the roadmap had it; or players first with builds a milestone later. | The operator asked what a build is beyond items and levels, and asked for the game to be judged from an owner's seat. Neither waits well for the other. The strands touch different packages, so three builders can work on them at once. |
| The rest of M1 is the tempo and density work (#315 to #324) plus what makes it a game to watch: 19 issues for a candidate, 8 more to close. #272 moves to M2 and #232 to M7; #288, #294 and #304 lose their milestone. | Keep all 36 open issues in M1.                                                                         | The operator's complaints are slow fights, harmless monsters, aimless residents and a page that doesn't fit. Hat bands, patch stitching and edge fades are none of those.                                                                 |
| A monster's numbers come from its level and rank by formula, with the expected gear of a resident of that level counted in. A region's tier is a band of monster levels.                                               | A hand-written table of hitpoints and damage per tier.                                                 | Two tables written apart disagreed by up to five times. With gear left out, a geared resident one tier short kills deeper fodder in one blow.                                                                                             |
| Experience is given per kill, `weight * (40 + M) / 40` rounded down, replacing 4 per hitpoint dealt (decision 0014). The experience table stays.                                                                       | Keep 4 per hitpoint; or a fixed number of kills per level.                                             | At blows of hundreds, 4 per hitpoint reaches 99 in an afternoon. This gives 99 in one skill in 7 to 9 weeks.                                                                                                                              |
| Each region has a monster budget from its tier, the time of day and the residents in it, at most 80. The crowd target (decision 0017) counts residents only, and the cap of three monsters a region goes.              | One cap for every region; or a budget that ignores residents.                                          | The operator asked for density, for caps scaled by residents with a hard ceiling, and for short refills.                                                                                                                                  |
| Sensors, blows and blocking look only at the actors of their own region, kept in a list per region. A region with no resident in it is dormant.                                                                        | Leave every check looking through all actors.                                                          | A tick was measured at 0.9 ms with 150 residents and 11.5 ms with 650, close to the square of the count. The full world would be 852 actors.                                                                                              |
| From M4, the first milestone past four regions, a viewer is sent what one frame draws: the actors on the ground in the frame with the fields a frame needs, a summary of every resident, and a count for each region.  | Keep sending the whole snapshot each tick (decision 0009).                                             | The whole world at 852 actors is 375 KB a tick. Players watch the stream and their own page, so only the machine that renders the stream needs the map.                                                                                   |
| The 13-week competitive period is a round. The calendar's year is 64 game days in four quarters.                                                                                                                       | "Season" for both.                                                                                     | Two proposals used the word for 13 weeks and one for 6 hours 24 minutes.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| One store, at Lantern Camp, which buys and sells for tally at a price set by its stock. Works draw from the same stock once it exists. No trade between two residents.                                                 | An automatic tithe to a store programs cannot sell to; an order book between residents.                | A work that drains the stock raises the price, which is how programs learn what the camp needs. An order book needs a counterparty and is a way to feed one account from another.                                                         |
| When hosting is raised is left to the operator (section 8 of the plan).                                                                                                                                                | During M4, as the roadmap had it.                                                                      | It is theirs to decide. The plan is ordered so that the milestones before the answer do not depend on it.                                                                                                                                 |

Checked for this: the formulas at levels 5, 18, 38, 58, 78 and 95, the pace
table, the budgets' totals, and the tick and snapshot timings in section 3.5
of the plan, taken on the shipped world at 15 to 650 residents. Not checked:
anything on screen, and a tick with more than three monsters a region, which
`main` cannot run. #316 measures that before the budgets are relied on.
