| `wander` stays in the actor's region. The Wanderer program strolls on the camp's map, and by day after every six strolls makes a trip with `walk_to` past its edge (the pinewood, the hills, the lower river in turn), strolls there three times and comes back. At nightfall it walks back to camp, and counts as back once it is within four tiles of `near_wood`, so a walk home that was cut short is taken up again. It gives up a chase after forty steps. | `wander` picks from the whole world for adventurers.                                                                          | A homebody's stroll would turn into a walk to the far end of the world. Trips are something a program chooses, which is what the design asks for. Only the wanderers leave the camp's map, so most of the cast stays in view: at 6 and 12 residents, seeds 1 and 2, residents are off the map 6 to 7% of the time. The chase limit came from a capture-test seed in which a wanderer followed a fleeing goblin for over ten minutes. |
| Monsters keep to their region: they never cross an exit. Threat sensors see only enemies in the same region, and blows stop at a region's edge.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Monsters roam, and fights reach across exits.                                                                                 | Caps and the crowd target are per region; a monster that walked into the next region would break both. The viewer still draws one map, the camp's region, with only the actors in it.
The page beside it lists every resident in the world, so a row stays put
while its resident is away; that row is faded and says which region they are
in. Someone walking off the map fades out over its last tile and is cut at
its rule. Drawing other regions and moving the view between them is #255.

Measured on the shipped world with 50 residents and the shipped monsters over
a game day (seed 1), residents per region (mean, then most at one time)
against its target: camp 47 and 50 (19), pinewood 1 and 7 (18), hills 1 and
6 (19), lower river 1 and 4 (17). The camp is over the target because only
the wanderers (a third of the cast) leave it, and only for a few strolls at a
time. The crowd target only governs monsters, so at 50 residents the camp got
none. Spreading residents further needs more places to live or different
programs; that is #272.
